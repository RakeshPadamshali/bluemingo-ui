PPC in plain words

PPC is the planning office of the steel plant. It takes what customers have ordered and decides what to make, in which mill, in what order and when. It then tells the melt shop what to cast and keeps every billet tied to the order it is meant for, until the steel is shipped.

PPC is its own module. It works beside the two applications the plant already runs, the production-confirmation application and the Allocation application, and uses only the services they already have, so they keep working exactly as they do today.

The journey of an order

Eight steps from order to dispatch

  1. Collect the orders. The planner uploads orders into the Order Book in the same Excel format used today, plus PPC's extra columns such as order category, priority, tolerance and PSN revision. PPC checks them and forwards them automatically to the production-confirmation application; the Allocation application receives its copy as it does today. The Order Book keeps a running count of how much is still to cast, to roll and to dispatch. A direct SAP feed comes later, once its contract exists.
  2. Set the month's target. The Monthly Plan says how many tonnes each mill should make this month, and blocks days when a product cannot be rolled, for example during a roll change.
  3. Group similar work. Changing a mill from one size to another takes time, so Campaign Planning groups orders into runs called campaigns: by product type for the bar mill, by input size for the blooming mill. It checks capacity and minimum quantities, and uses stock that already exists before asking for new steel.
  4. Order the steel. The Casting Indent says what to cast: grade, section, length, how many billets per heat, and which lines are top priority. PPC loads it into our MES as the casting plan, which creates the planned heats and their orders, and sends it to SMS MES.
  5. Bring in what SMS cast. SMS MES reports each heat it cast and the billets cut from it. SMS Batch Arrival matches each real heat to its planned heat and replays it in our MES, so every billet exists with its heat and its order.
  6. Agree the day's order of work. In Rolling Sequence, the mill team proposes the order of sizes and PPC accepts it, after checking material and rolls. Release sends the rolling programme through the existing upload and saves the running order on the floor.
  7. Charge only the right billets. Operators charge on the existing Charging screen. Only the billets PPC released can be charged; for a make-to-order block that means only the billets pegged to that order. Scanning the billet label can be switched on per installation. PPC's Charging Pool shows what each block allows.
  8. Finish and ship. Finishing schedules plan grinding, annealing, GMM and bright-bar work. The bars are dispatched from the production-confirmation application, and SAP is told once its interface exists.
Working with what exists

How PPC hands over to the existing applications

The production-confirmation application and the Allocation application stay as they are. PPC uses their existing services, the same ones their screens use, and keeps everything new in its own data. Four small changes are requested, and each can stay switched off where it is not needed.

Helpers

What runs alongside

Swapping

Moves a billet from one order to another when plans change. PPC checks the rules and keeps the log; the Allocation application does the re-allocation; billets already produced follow through the bridge. Every swap can be reversed.

Order holds

The PPC Super User holds an order, part of it, or a schedule. PPC puts each of its billets on hold with the existing billet hold, so nothing of it can be charged or confirmed. Part of a hold can be released, by billets or by tonnage not yet made. Holds placed in SAP or SFDC, for example by Marketing, show who placed them and release when the source releases them.

Job work

Sends billets to an outside vendor on a PPC challan, holds them "At vendor", and on return records the vendor's work as a production step, with losses. Material can come back in several part receipts: each one is checked for loss only on the share it covers, and the rest stays "At vendor".

WIP visibility

Shows where the steel is at every stage, whether each billet is gross, net or ground, what is still arriving, and which orders are about to run short. It also lists the free FG stock with no customer order: how long it has waited, why it is free, and the open orders it fits.

Ferro-alloy plan

Works out how much nickel, chrome and other alloys the melt shop needs for the planned grades, and flags any shortfall.

Dashboard and interfaces

Plan against actual, on-time delivery and balances by stage, plus the state of every hand-off: order forwarding, the last Allocation pull, and the interfaces still waiting.

Example

One order, start to finish

A customer orders 480 t of 20MnCr5 round bar, RO 32.

  1. The planner uploads the order in PPC. PPC forwards it, and both applications have it within a minute. The Order Book shows 480 t still to cast, roll and dispatch.
  2. Campaign Planning puts it in a bar-mill campaign of RO 32 sizes, with other orders that share the size.
  3. The Casting Indent asks for square 160 billets, 12 m long: 10 billets and one shorter last piece per 25 t heat. It is loaded as the casting plan and sent to SMS MES.
  4. SMS casts heat 2627-01172. PPC replays it, and its 11 billets now exist in our MES, each tied to the order.
  5. The billets become block 1 of the rolling sequence on 05 Oct, 06:00 to 12:00. Release sends the programme and saves the running order.
  6. Operators charge them on the existing screen, where only these billets are allowed. They are rolled, ground and shipped, and the order's balance drops to zero.
