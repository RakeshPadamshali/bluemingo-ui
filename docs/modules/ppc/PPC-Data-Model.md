## 0. Organisation

### 0.1 Ownership and landing
PPC is a **separate module**, built like the Quality module (amended 22-Oct-2026 — section 21): its tables carry the prefix `mes_ppc_` and its views `v_ppc_`; they are created by PPC's own migration history, as the Quality module's `mes_qc_*` tables are by its own, and PPC has its own screens and backend. PPC **reads** the shared database freely (read-only) — orders, lots, queues, confirmations, the Quality module's decisions and the Allocation application's mirrored plans. It **writes** to the production-confirmation application only through the services that application's own screens call — order upload, rolling-program import and resequence, casting-plan upload, lot hold and release, operation assignment, production confirmation — as a named PPC user where the service has a user field; the application has no authentication on any endpoint (a risk noted; PPC calls it as a system user). It writes to the Allocation application only through that application's existing services, with a PPC client account (setup, not code). Production-confirmation tables are **never extended**: PPC keeps its own fields in `mes_ppc_*` side tables keyed by the existing ids (R-PPC-117, section 21); orders stay in `mes_orders` / `mes_order_line_items`, the single order record. Where no existing service can do what PPC needs, it is either not automated or it becomes a change request that is **off by default**, so another installation of the application sees no difference: there are four, **PPC-R-32 … PPC-R-35** in `Requests-PPC.md`, and every earlier request PPC-R-01 … PPC-R-31 is withdrawn into the module. The Allocation application receives **no** request (ALC-R-05 … ALC-R-08 withdrawn, 21.10). Section headings carry the owner tag PPC (PLATFORM before 22-Oct-2026). Nothing is QA-owned except the read of PSN data (CQ design) and the pit-cooling / grinding rules (MDM).

### 0.2 Conventions
As the QA Data-Model §2 and the MDM design §0.2: PK `<entity>_id bigint`; **`+ audit tail`**; `(Y)` nullable; scope keys and resolution rule **R-S**; `effective_from/to` informational until P3. Product-agnostic per D0: units (SMS, BRM, BLM, ABGM, Annealing, GMM, Bright Bar) are `mes_operations` / `mes_factories` rows; product types, order categories, SAP order types, stages and constraint types are data. "Stage" = a production step of the order's routing (cast, roll, downstream, FGHO) resolved from `mes_operations`. **References to the production-confirmation application (amended 22-Oct-2026 — R-PPC-117):** a PPC column naming one of its rows carries that row's id; a lot is named by `inventory_id` + `material_number` with **no foreign key**, because the application hard-deletes lots, and a queue row (schedule material, child) by its id with no foreign key, because the Allocation pull rebuilds queue rows — a PPC constraint must never block or change what the application does. Stable rows (orders and lines, operations, equipment, masters) keep their foreign keys.

### 0.3 Live facts that shaped the design (verified 2026-09-15; corrected 22-Oct-2026)
- `mes_order_line_items` carries `order_type` (all rows MTO), `status` (all RELEASED), `pending_quantity`, `pending_nop`, `routing_id`, `delivery_date`, `tdc_no` (NULL everywhere); `mes_orders.order_source` = Local; the CQ pass adds `tdc_id` + `psn_revision_no` (CQ-R-02). *(Open since 22-Oct-2026: the pin is kept on `mes_ppc_order_line` while production-confirmation tables are not extended — 21.13.)*
- **The live order link of a lot** is `mes_schedule_material_allocations` (child × order line × quantity, written by the plan dump from the Allocation pull, with the `mes_schedule_material_childs.order_line_id` copy) and `mes_order_inventory` (lot × order line, written at confirmation from that allocation; deactivate-never-delete). `mes_batch_order_allocation` and `mes_inventory_order_allocation` are **empty and unused** — 0 rows, never written, no view, function or trigger, not read by the Quality module — and the design no longer uses them. No existing service adds or moves a link (only the deallocate that salvage uses), and each Allocation pull rebuilds the links of the heats not yet generated *(corrected 22-Oct-2026 — 21.2; the earlier "batch ↔ order-line allocation table is the anchor for swapping" is withdrawn)*.
- `mes_schedules` (factory, operation, equipment, `is_hold`, `charge_status`) → `mes_schedule_materials` (plan_id, source_system / source_ref / pulled_at from the Allocation pull) → `mes_schedule_material_childs` (planned_seq, planned_equipment_id, stage_item_code, pieces) is the execution queue the Confirmation screen reads; Manual Sequencing re-orders it; the Allocation pull marked ADHOC / TEMPORARY builds it from released heats. The pull is the only source of queue rows — the rolling-program import stores reference rows only *(corrected 22-Oct-2026 — 21.1)*; `is_hold` and the order-line hold are stored but enforced nowhere, while the lot hold (`mes_inventory_holds`) is enforced *(21.4)*.
- `mes_pln_details` (478 plans) and `mes_pln_*` mirror the external planner's output — reference only (F7.13-02).
- The casting-plan upload sizes heats per BPL from the uploaded sheet since 07-Oct-2026 — Approx. Ton. ÷ No. of Heats, whole billets, tolerance = the casting operation's `heat_size_tolerance_pct` (default 10 %) — with **no fixed 25 t** (`CastingPlanHeatBuilder`; corrected 22-Oct-2026 — 21.5); heat generation and the casting reconciliation (`mes_production_allocation_deviation`: FULL / PARTIAL / OVER / NONE) exist; there is **no casting indent object** — the plan comes in by Excel.
- `mes_inventory` carries `prev_operation` / `next_operation`, `state`, `ud_status`, `routing_complete` — **no equipment and no yard location**; `mes_material_sequence.planned_equipment_id` is the only equipment hint.
- Consumable actuals per confirmation exist (`mes_consumable_usage`, auto-calculated from norms); `mes_product_order_forecast` is an empty container.
- No swap object on either surface; the Allocation application's `Mst_Compatible_Value` and attribute match engine serve allocation, and `mes_audit_log` carries ALLOCATION_RELEASE events.
- The rolling-program tables are `mes_operation_program_upload` / `mes_operation_program`, renamed from `mes_rolling_program_upload` / `mes_rolling_program` when the programme became per operation *(corrected 22-Oct-2026 — 21.1)*.
- The production-confirmation application has **no authentication** on any endpoint *(22-Oct-2026)* — a risk noted; PPC calls it as a system user (R-PPC-116).

## 1. Cross-cutting decisions for PPC

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **M-PPC-01** | **One order book, one line table.** The SAP sales order, its production order and the MES production orders are rows of `mes_orders` / `mes_order_line_items` / `mes_ppc_production_order`; internal, ISO, NCO, trial and development orders are the same rows with an `order_category`; ZSDP dispatch orders are lines with `planning_relevant = false`. *(Amended 22-Oct-2026 — 2.1, 21.7: PPC's fields sit beside those rows in `mes_ppc_order` / `mes_ppc_order_line`; orders PPC creates reach `mes_orders` through its forwarding.)* | BRD OMB-002/005/006, 7.1.3: one source of truth for planning, BTP, allocation and campaigns; the built order screen, import and pegging keep working. |
| **M-PPC-02** | **Balances are a computed snapshot per line per stage** (`mes_ppc_order_stage_balance`), refreshed per shift and on events, not derived on the fly by every screen. | OMB-007/010: BTC / BTR / BTP / BTD / OTIF by stage with yield factors; one place every screen and report reads. |
| **M-PPC-03** | **Allocation stays where it is; swapping is a new transaction over it.** A swap re-points `mes_batch_order_allocation` rows (and the lot / schedule pegging) in one transaction, records every line with its rule evaluation, and is reversible as a new transaction. *(Amended 22-Oct-2026 — 21.2: PPC decides and logs the swap; the Allocation application executes it through its existing services and the swap bridge, PPC-R-32, moves a produced lot's `mes_order_inventory` link; `mes_batch_order_allocation` is empty and unused.)* | 7.2.2: five scenarios, auto-swap, reverse, log — none exists; the pegging tables do. |
| **M-PPC-04** | **Campaign and rolling schedule are distinct artefacts** (SEQ-009): the campaign is the multi-day, multi-product commitment; the rolling schedule is the size sequence for a day / shift that releases into the built execution queue (`mes_schedules`). *(Amended 22-Oct-2026 — 21.1: release posts the rolling programme and saves the running order of the lots the Allocation pull has queued.)* | The built queue and sequencing are kept as the execution layer. |
| **M-PPC-05** | **Constraints are configuration inside MES** `[assumption — D-02]`: a constraint master per unit with type, parameters, precedence and enforcement; feasibility results are stored per campaign order and schedule block; the planner's plan remains a reference tab. | BRD 7.3.2 CST-001, F7.13-02 (MES runs without FP). |
| **M-PPC-06** | **The SMS → Mills batch feed is a staging table with idempotent upsert** into the built schedule / inventory tables; the Allocation pull's read model is reused as the first feed `[assumption — D-07]`. *(Amended 22-Oct-2026 — 21.11: SMS MES reports the actual heats and PPC replays them through existing services; no upsert into platform tables, no scheduled pull.)* | MAT-001/002: near-real-time, validated, with discrepancy flags; the pull is ADHOC today. |

| **M-PPC-08** | **Engines deferred, content designed:** approvals (HOD, PPC-HOD finalisation, super-user holds) are named actions on the MDM privileged-action master; alerts are events; SAP messages are staging rows with status. *(Amended 22-Oct-2026 — 21.9: SAP waits for a contract; then one PPC outbox.)* | Scope decision 2026-09-12. |
| **M-PPC-09** | **Masters come from the MDM design** (length, yield, family / section load, MoQ, swap rules, customer priority, order-book rules, ferro-alloy norms, annealing load, form conversion, batch derivation, pit cooling, grinding). No PPC-local copy. | One book of record. |
| **M-PPC-10** | **PPC is a separate module** (22-Oct-2026): its own tables `mes_ppc_*` in its own migration history; it reads the shared database and writes to the production-confirmation and Allocation applications only through their existing services; side tables keyed by the existing ids instead of new columns; four change requests, each off by default or behaviour-identical. | The production-confirmation and Allocation applications run unchanged at another client's installation (section 21). |

### 2.2 `mes_ppc_production_order` — MES production orders split from the SAP order *(rows 4, 14; BRM-011, OMB-012)* — PPC

- Confirmation is recorded against the queue row as today and PPC attributes it to the released MES production order through `mes_ppc_queue_link` (BRM-011; amended 22-Oct-2026); the sum of active production orders of a line may not exceed `release_qty` (R-PPC-03). ### 2.3 `mes_ppc_order_stage_balance` — BTC / BTR / BTP / BTD / OTIF by stage *(row 3; OMB-007…011, BRM-010, F7.1-01, F7.11-01, F7.6-03)* — PPC
- **`mes_ppc_order_stage_balance`** — `balance_id` PK · `order_line_id` FK · **`stage_operation_id`** FK `mes_operations` (SMS cast / BRM or BLM roll / ABGM / Annealing / GMM / Bright Bar / FGHO — the stages of the line's routing) · `stage_code varchar(20)` (CAST / ROLL / PROCESS / FGHO — data label) · `order_qty numeric(18,4)` · `release_qty numeric(18,4)` (Y) · `produced_qty numeric(18,4)` (confirmed at this stage) · `wip_qty numeric(18,4)` (in process at this stage) · `upstream_wip_qty numeric(18,4)` (material pegged to the line at earlier stages, yield-adjusted) · `rejected_qty numeric(18,4)` · `diverted_qty numeric(18,4)` · `inter_plant_qty numeric(18,4)` (Y — STO material expected, OMB-011; counted only under the INCOMING_COVER rule, R-PPC-100) · `purchased_qty numeric(18,4)` (Y — accepted bought or transferred lots pegged to the line at this stage, R-PPC-100) · `fg_qty numeric(18,4)` · `fgho_qty numeric(18,4)` · `dispatched_qty numeric(18,4)` (reference) · `yield_factor numeric(7,4)` (from the MDM yield master, resolved for the stage) · **`balance_qty numeric(18,4)`** (the BTC / BTR / BTP of the stage) · `balance_pieces int` (Y) · `computed_at timestamptz` · `computed_by varchar(20)` (SHIFT_JOB / EVENT / MANUAL) · **+ audit tail**. UQ (`order_line_id`, `stage_operation_id`).
- **R-PPC-02 — balance formula (BRM-010, 7.5 table):** for a stage S, `balance = (order_qty or release_qty when set) − produced_at_S − purchased_at_S + rejected_at_S + diverted_out_at_S − upstream_wip_covering_S`, where `upstream_wip_covering_S` = Σ (pegged upstream quantity × cumulative yield from its stage to S) and FGHO uses `fgho_qty` (not dispatch, OMB-008); `purchased_at_S` = accepted bought or transferred lots pegged to the line at S (R-PPC-100). Worked example (7.5): SO 100 t, cast 90 t, rejected / diverted 15 t → **BTC 25 t**; with 10 t of pegged as-cast WIP at 96 % gross yield covering the roll stage, BTR = 100 − 0 + 0 − 9.6 = 90.4 t. Refresh every shift (a scheduled job of the PPC service) and on confirmation, UD, swap, amendment and inter-plant receipt events.
- **R-PPC-04 — OTIF:** `otif_status` from the order-book rules (MDM OTIF_WINDOW): ON_TRACK when the projected FGHO date (from the campaign / schedule) is within the window, AT_RISK when the balance cannot be covered by released schedules before the window, LATE when the window has passed with balance > 0, DELIVERED_OTIF / DELIVERED_LATE after FGHO.

### 19.1 Production scenario — make-to-order and make-to-stock *(points 8, 17, 35, 36, 37, 38, 44, 58, 124, 125, 131; conflict 1)* — PPC

- **R-PPC-46 — scenario per order type:** the existing order-line `order_type` (Enquiry / MTO / MTS — stored at upload, read by nothing today) carries the production scenario; its default comes from the order-book rule PRODUCTION_SCENARIO for the order type (Master Data order-book rule, scope order type; seed: every type MTO); PPC may change it until the line is released. - **R-PPC-47 — make-to-stock material stays free:** a make-to-stock demand reaches the casting indent as a grade / PSN / size quantity (indent line `demand_source = MTS_STOCK`, no order line). - **R-PPC-48 — pegging before release (amended 22-Oct-2026 — 21.2, 21.3):** a free lot is pegged to a matching order line before release — the FREE_TO_SO rules (PSN, grade, section and input size blocking; length soft), best slot score first — and the peg is executed as a FREE_TO_SO swap through the Allocation application, the swap bridge giving a produced lot its first order (R-PPC-123). The lot takes the order line's route from the stage it stands at (R-PPC-77).
- **R-PPC-49 — ISO order before release (amended 22-Oct-2026 — 21.3):** a make-to-stock lot PPC puts into a release without a matching order is booked to an ISO order created by PPC before the release (category ISO; grade, PSN, size and length from the lot; forwarded through the order upload, 21.7) and pegged to it as in R-PPC-48, so every charged lot carries an order; it stays free for the ISO_TO_SO swap (R-PPC-09). - **R-PPC-50 — charging pool, a mode per mill (amended 16-Oct-2026 and 22-Oct-2026 — 21.3):** the mode is the mill's CHARGING_POOL_MODE constraint row and decides which billets PPC's release sequences; with the existing per-operation setting `require_sequencing_before_charge` switched on, the production-confirmation application charges only sequenced lots (enforced server-side, unchanged). ORDER_SCOPED_MTO (seed for BRM and BLM, BRD MAT-006 / 007) sequences for a make-to-order block only the lots pegged to its order lines — any other lot comes in only through a swap before release, swap rules checked and logged — and for a make-to-stock block every eligible cleared lot PPC chose, so a planning omission still cannot strand material (the deliberate 31-Aug-2026 reason). ALL_CLEARED keeps today's behaviour — every cleared lot, pegged first (with the setting off nothing changes); ORDER_SCOPED sequences only pegged lots for every block. Σ allocated ≤ batch quantity is a hard check wherever an allocation is made — PPC checks it before it asks the Allocation application to release (Batch Allocation, Swapping, campaign attach; the charge scan is withdrawn, amended 22-Oct-2026). At confirmation an excess is warned and logged, never refused — the application's rule that a confirmation does not fail on a secondary record, with no database constraint. How a charge splits a lot with several allocations is the application's existing behaviour, unchanged (the piece-wise apportioning once asked in PPC-R-19 is withdrawn, 22-Oct-2026). ## 21. PPC as a separate module (22-Oct-2026)

The production-confirmation application and the Allocation application stay **unchanged** — another client runs them as they are. PPC therefore becomes a **separate module**, like the Quality module. Every touch point between PPC and the two applications was re-checked read-only in their code (and in the Quality module's) and decided with the user point by point on 21–22 Oct 2026. Rules continue from R-PPC-115; the paragraphs this section supersedes are amended in place.

- **R-PPC-116 — the module boundary:** PPC's tables are `mes_ppc_*` and its views `v_ppc_*`, in its own migration history, with its own screens and backend. PPC reads the shared database freely (read-only) and writes only through existing services — the endpoints the applications' own screens use — as a named PPC user where a user field exists. The production-confirmation application has no authentication on any endpoint (code): a risk noted; PPC calls it as a system user. The Allocation application validates its callers' tokens; PPC calls it with its own client account (setup, not code). New data never goes into production-confirmation tables — no new columns there. Where no existing service can do what PPC needs, it is either not automated or it becomes a change request that is off by default, so the other installation sees no difference (PPC-R-32 … PPC-R-35).
- **R-PPC-117 — side tables and references:** PPC keeps its fields in side tables keyed by the existing ids. `mes_ppc_order` and `mes_ppc_order_line` are 1:1 with `mes_orders` and `mes_order_line_items` (primary key = the existing id) and carry the order code, line # and schedule line the order upload matches on; a row is written once PPC's forward has created or matched the order (21.7), and an order that reached `mes_orders` without PPC — through the existing upload, until PPC's order management is built — gets its rows when PPC first reads it, with the defaults of the order-book rules. A lot is referenced by `inventory_id` + `material_number` with no foreign key (the application hard-deletes lots) and a queue row by its id with no foreign key (the Allocation pull rebuilds queue rows); stable rows keep their foreign keys. Production-confirmation screens never show PPC fields; PPC screens join them.

### 21.1 Release to the production queue *(touch point 1)* — PPC
- Facts (code): the rolling-program import `POST /api/rolling-program/import` (file and operation) stores **reference rows only** in `mes_operation_program_upload` / `mes_operation_program`; the screen then saves the order automatically through `POST /api/schedule/operations/{op}/resequence`. The queue rows come from the Allocation pull (plan dump), not from the import. Row order = running order (`excel_row`). No idempotency — the same file posted twice writes an empty SUCCESS log; the planner's last save wins.
- **R-PPC-118 — release = programme upload + saved order:** when a rolling schedule is RELEASED (after the ACCEPT round, R-PPC-22), PPC, per mill operation: (1) builds the sheet in the exact published layout — first sheet, row 1 headers Date, F Size, S Size, Grade, Heat No., Rec Size, Actual Length, RCS Cutting Length, Ch.No., Ch.Date, Finish Length, Job No., Rec Pcs, Rec Wt (MT), Category, Inspection Remarks, UID, ED Date, Execution, HTCondition — one row per pegged billet of the released blocks in block order (the row order is the running order), only the billets each block allows (CHARGING_POOL_MODE, R-PPC-124), named `PPC-REL-<schedule>-v<n>.xlsx`; (2) posts it to the import as the PPC user (`mes_ppc_program_upload` SENT); (3) reads the import's rejected rows and the upload row it wrote → ACCEPTED, PARTIAL or FAILED, with `rejected_rows_json` and `platform_upload_id`; (4) reads the operation's queue and the programme lots; (5) saves the order and the mills through resequence with the programme lots in block order (`resequence_status` SAVED) and writes one `mes_ppc_queue_link` row per lot — RELEASED, with its sheet row, saved position, block, production order and queue-row ids; (6) sets the production orders RELEASED and raises PPC_BLOCK_RELEASED per block (R-PPC-115). PPC never posts the same file twice: before a post or a retry it looks for its file name among the uploads, and an upload found is read back instead. Fallback: the planner downloads the same file and uploads it on the existing screen (`delivery_mode` DOWNLOADED) — the screen saves the order itself and PPC records the links at its next read.
- **R-PPC-119 — rejected rows and lots not queued:** a row the import rejects is listed on the schedule with its block, lot and reason (PPC_RELEASE_UPLOAD_REJECTED); its lot is left out of the saved order — with `require_sequencing_before_charge` on it cannot be charged — until the planner corrects the cause and releases a new version (a new file) or drops the lot from the block. A lot in the sheet but not in the queue — its heat not pulled yet — is DROPPED with the reason "not in the queue" and listed by the pull watch (R-PPC-141; PPC_QUEUE_LOT_DROPPED).
- **R-PPC-120 — moves seen at the next read:** PPC reads the queue every PPC_QUEUE_READ_MINUTES (seed 5) and compares each lot's position with `released_seq`: a move within the block is free (R-PPC-29a); a move into another block's range or another mill is MOVED_ACROSS_BLOCK — change log (RESEQUENCE) and PPC_SCHEDULE_CHANGED (R-PPC-26); a charged lot becomes CHARGED (from the charge records); a lot that left the queue uncharged — held, reassigned or deleted — becomes DROPPED with the reason.
- Worked example — a release with one rejected row: BRM Schedule 7 v1 is released with three blocks of 14, 16 and 12 pegged billets. PPC posts `PPC-REL-BRM-S7-v1.xlsx` with 42 rows in block order; the import rejects row 17 (block 2, a billet of heat 2526-16496, with the reason the import returns) and the upload is PARTIAL. PPC reads the queue — the 41 accepted lots are there — and saves positions 1–41 through resequence: block 1 at 1–14, block 2 at 15–29, block 3 at 30–41. Queue links: 41 RELEASED, 1 DROPPED ("rejected by the import"); the schedule shows the rejected row. The planner corrects the billet's data and releases v2: PPC posts `PPC-REL-BRM-S7-v2.xlsx`, the row is accepted, PPC saves the 42 positions again and the link turns RELEASED.
- Correction: "release writes schedule materials / children" (R-PPC-29 as first written) was wrong — release orders the lots the pull has already queued.

### 21.2 Swap *(touch point 2)* — PPC
- Facts (code): the live order link is `mes_schedule_material_allocations.order_line_id` (from the pull / plan dump) and `mes_order_inventory` (written at confirmation from that allocation). `mes_batch_order_allocation` and `mes_inventory_order_allocation` are empty and unused (0 rows, never written, no view, function or trigger, not used by the Quality module). No existing service adds or moves a link — only the deallocate that salvage uses. Each Allocation pull rebuilds the links of the heats not yet generated. The Allocation application releases (allocate), un-releases (deallocate) and forbids a batch for a line (set-release-block); it has no "give batch X to line B" action — its engine proposes.
- **R-PPC-121 — PPC decides, the Allocation application executes:** PPC owns the swap decision, its rules and its log (`mes_ppc_swap_transaction`, `mes_ppc_swap_line` — rule evaluation, soft-mismatch override and stage on the line). Each line runs through four existing services of the Allocation application: (1) `POST /api/ui/deallocate` — Un-Release X from A; (2) `POST /api/ui/set-release-block` — forbid X for A; (3) `POST /api/allocation/run` scoped by `orderLineIds` [B]; (4) if the run proposed X for B, `POST /api/ui/allocate` — Release; otherwise PPC shows the planner the engine's proposal (`engine_proposal_json`) to accept — the proposal is released instead — or to cancel — PPC undoes steps 1 and 2 through the same services. Each step's outcome is kept on the line (`step_status` and the plan references); a failed step stops the line (FAILED, `failed_step`, PPC_SWAP_STEP_FAILED) and the planner retries it or reverses the steps done.
- **R-PPC-122 — the swap bridge for produced lots:** the Allocation pull never touches a heat once generated, so a produced lot's link moves through the swap bridge (PPC-R-32, off by default). After step 4 PPC calls it; the bridge cross-checks the release through the allocator read connection the application already has and, in one transaction, deactivates the lot's active `mes_order_inventory` row for A and adds one for B (history kept), re-points the lot's remaining `mes_schedule_material_allocations` rows and the `mes_schedule_material_childs.order_line_id` copy, recomputes pending for A and B and writes `mes_audit_log` with the PPC swap number (`bridge_status` DONE, `bridge_audit_ref`). It is a no-op when the lot is already on B (NO_OP) and refuses a lot consumed, dispatched or on hold, or a closed target line (REFUSED with the reason; PPC_SWAP_BRIDGE_REFUSED). A heat not yet generated needs no bridge (NOT_NEEDED) — the next pull carries the new link. While the bridge is off, PPC offers swaps only for heats not yet generated. PPC then adds `diverted_qty` on A and recomputes the stage balances of both lines.
- **R-PPC-123 — reverse, whole lots, first order:** a REVERSE transaction runs the same steps the other way (B → A) and calls the bridge again; both directions stay in `mes_order_inventory`, which never deletes. Swaps move whole lots (part-lot swap: OPEN-PPC-16). Pegging a free lot is a FREE_TO_SO line with steps 3 and 4 only, and the bridge gives a produced lot its **first order** — used for bought billets and blooms (R-PPC-133), migrated stock (R-PPC-53) and ISO orders before release (R-PPC-125); `source_ref` names where the lot came from.
- Worked example — swap X from A to B: lot X (heat 2526-16496, 10 billets, 24.1 t) is pegged to SO-88012 / 10 (A); PPC swaps it to SO-91562 / 20 (B), transaction SW-0412, stage SEQUENCING, rules passed. Step 1 un-releases X's plan entry for A; step 2 forbids X for A; step 3 runs allocation for B only and the engine proposes X (24.1 t) for B; step 4 releases it. X's heat is generated, so PPC calls the bridge: the `mes_order_inventory` row of X for A goes INACTIVE and a new ACTIVE row for B is added, X's remaining schedule-allocation rows and child copies move to B, A's pending rises by 24.1 t and B's falls by 24.1 t, and the audit entry carries SW-0412 — line DONE, bridge DONE. Had the engine proposed lot Y (23.8 t) for B instead, the planner would see "engine proposes Y for B" and either accept (Y is released to B; X stays un-released and forbidden for A, so free) or cancel (X goes back to A).

### 21.3 Charging *(touch point 3)* — PPC
- Facts (code): the charging pool has no order filter and no scan path; the existing per-operation setting `require_sequencing_before_charge` (off by default) withholds every unsequenced lot from charging, enforced server-side. It was switched off on 31-Aug-2026 because a planning omission stranded material (an RCS routed back for double rolling, cleared but not sequenced); the setting stays, per operation.
- **R-PPC-124 — charging follows what PPC sequences:** one Charging screen for both clients. JSW switches the existing setting on for its BRM and BLM charging operations; PPC's release (R-PPC-118) sequences only the billets allowed, by the mill's CHARGING_POOL_MODE row in `mes_ppc_scheduling_constraint`: ORDER_SCOPED_MTO (seed BRM and BLM) — for a make-to-order block the billets pegged to its order lines, for a make-to-stock block the cleared billets PPC chose; ORDER_SCOPED — pegged billets only; ALL_CLEARED — every cleared billet, pegged first. With the setting off — the other installation — nothing changes.
- **R-PPC-125 — before release, not at the furnace:** swaps happen before release (21.2); the swap button at the furnace and the ISO order at charging are dropped (R-PPC-48, R-PPC-49 and R-PPC-52 amended): a billet PPC puts into an MTS block without an order gets its ISO order before release — created by PPC, forwarded (21.7) and pegged through the Allocation application and the bridge (R-PPC-123). The risk of a forgotten MTS billet is lowered by releasing every eligible MTS billet and every cleared pegged lot of the released blocks, double-rolled RCS included, and by listing cleared lots that are in no release (PPC_FREE_BATCHES_EXIST). A forced charge of another lot (FORCED_CHARGE_OVERRIDE, with a reason) is added to the release by PPC.
- **R-PPC-126 — Charging Pool view and scan to charge:** PPC's former Charging Scan & Peg screen becomes the read-only **Charging Pool** view — what each block allows, the MTS pool and the charges seen in the charge records; charging itself happens on the production-confirmation Charging screen. The scan box there is PPC-R-33 (installation-level setting, off by default): the scanned lot is selected from the list and charged as today through the same service, or the screen says why it is not chargeable — not in today's sequence, not cleared by Quality, already charged, not at this mill.

### 21.4 Order holds *(touch point 4)* — PPC
- Facts (code): the schedule `is_hold` and the order-line hold are stored but enforced nowhere; the lot hold (`POST /api/inventory/{id}/holds`, `/holds/release`) is enforced — the lot leaves every queue and cannot be charged or confirmed; one open hold per lot; a release returns the lot to AVAILABLE.
- **R-PPC-127 — an order hold is a set of lot holds:** an order hold by the PPC Super User (R-PPC-102) holds, through the existing lot hold, every lot of its scope — ORDER (every line), LINE, PART (chosen lots) or SCHEDULE (the lots of one schedule) — with the hold-reason master row "Order hold (PPC)" and the order hold's reason and number in the remark; one `mes_ppc_hold_lot` row per lot, APPLIED with the platform hold id, or PENDING while a refused call is retried. The scope UNRELEASED_BALANCE holds no lot: it stops PPC from raising the release quantity (R-PPC-103).
- **R-PPC-128 — skip, hold later, release only PPC's holds:** a lot already held for another reason is skipped (SKIPPED_OTHER_HOLD, PPC_HOLD_LOT_SKIPPED) and held by PPC as soon as that hold is released (PPC_HOLD_LOT_REAPPLIED); lots pegged to the held scope later — by a pull, a swap or a cutting confirmation — are held as they appear. Releasing the order hold releases only the holds that carry PPC's reason (RELEASED) and the lot returns to AVAILABLE; other holds stay.
- **R-PPC-129 — the Allocation application and the line status:** while the hold is open, PPC blocks the held order's plan entries in the Allocation application through `POST /api/ui/set-release-block` and unblocks them on release (`alloc_block_state`) — this replaces ALC-R-08. The line status ON_HOLD that PPC forwards through the order upload is display only (enforced nowhere — code); PPC remembers the earlier status (`status_before_hold`) and forwards it back on release (21.7).
- Worked example: the PPC Super User holds SO-88012 (lines 10 and 20, reason "Customer credit hold"). Nine lots are pegged: seven are held (APPLIED) and two already carry a Quality hold (SKIPPED_OTHER_HOLD); the order's plan entries are blocked. The next day Quality releases one of its holds and PPC holds that lot at its next read; two billets cut for line 20 that evening are held as they appear. On release PPC lifts its ten holds; the remaining lot keeps its Quality hold.

### 21.5 Heat size *(touch point 5)* — PPC
- Facts (code): since 07-Oct-2026 the heat builder sizes heats per BPL from the casting-plan sheet (Approx. Ton. ÷ No. of Heats; tolerance = the casting operation's `heat_size_tolerance_pct`, default 10 %); there is no fixed 25 t.
- **R-PPC-130 — PPC fills the casting-plan sheet:** from its casting indent (planned heats with their orders) and the caster's HEAT_SIZE_T, PPC fills the existing casting-plan sheet — headers on row 5, BPL No and No. of Heats merged per block, Approx. Ton. = heats × HEAT_SIZE_T — and posts it through the existing casting-plan upload (`/api/casting-plan`); the builder sizes the heats from the sheet, unchanged. Billets per heat (R-PPC-110) follow from the same HEAT_SIZE_T. It works for JSW once PPC-R-35 removes the fixed client codes from the casting-plan import; when SMS MES plans the heats, the builder is not involved (21.11). Correction: "the heat builder reads HEAT_SIZE_T instead of its fixed 25 t" is dropped from PPC-R-28 and R-PPC-110.
- Worked example: indent block BPL 1 — 304L SQ 160, three heats for SO-88012 / 10, HEAT_SIZE_T 25 t → Approx. Ton. 75; the builder sizes 75 ÷ 3 = 25 t per heat within ± 10 %; each heat gives 10 billets of 12 m and a 4,403 mm last piece (R-PPC-110).

### 21.6 Code state, route, bought billets and blooms *(touch point 6)* — PPC
- **R-PPC-131 — code state derived (A):** nothing stores GROSS / NET / G&D (code). PPC derives it read-only in `v_ppc_lot_code_state`: GROSS = an SMS lot not yet cleared; NET = an SMS lot cleared by Quality, from the Quality module's decision history, since `ud_status` is overwritten; G&D = the lot's last operation is the grinding operation — ABGM must exist as an operation (master data); a hot-out lot keeps its code. Shown on PPC screens and supplied to SAP and reports later; the production-confirmation screens do not show it.
- **R-PPC-132 — the route kept by PPC (B):** a lot's next operation today is the operator's pick (allowed everywhere) or the order line's single linear routing (code). PPC keeps each lot's route in `mes_ppc_material_route`, `mes_ppc_material_route_stage` and `mes_ppc_lot_route`; it sets the order line's routing where the route is linear (through the routing attribute rules the order upload applies, 18); after each confirmation it compares the lot's next operation with the operation of the route's next stage and flags a deviation (PPC_ROUTE_DEVIATION); the planner corrects the lot through `POST /api/operation-assignment/assign` — which also shows it on the Bright Bar assignment screen (code) — or accepts the deviation with a reason. Limit: one route per order line can be preset.
- **R-PPC-133 — bought billets and blooms (C):** PPC-R-34 creates the lot at the mill's charging stage when the Quality module accepts a purchased or customer-supplied billet or bloom receipt (off by default; today no receipt creates a lot, and the only lot creator, the inventory upload, is wired to the other installation — code); the lot's order comes through the Allocation application and the bridge (R-PPC-123). PPC records receipt ↔ lot ↔ order on the first-order swap line (`source_ref`) and counts the lot in the stage balance (`purchased_qty`, R-PPC-100).

### 21.7 Order management — PPC's order upload and forwarding *(SAP order intake)* — PPC
- Facts (code): orders enter the production-confirmation application only through its Order Data upload `POST /api/orders/import` (.xlsx; 15 fixed columns and one per active order attribute — `GET /api/orders/upload-columns`). After each upload the application copies the orders to the Allocation application (insert and update only: one-way, the production-confirmation application is the master), and the upload aborts when that copy cannot be made. A re-upload matches the header on the order code and the lines on order, line # and schedule line, overwrites every field (blanks clear) and rewrites the attributes; the rows of an order released in the planner are rejected ("Released — skipped (locked)"); the statuses are CREATED / RELEASED / IN_PROGRESS / COMPLETED / BLOCKED / ON_HOLD — "closed" becomes COMPLETED, an unknown value silently CREATED; there is no cancel, short-close or single-line delete, and the previous status is not kept after a hold. The SKU is derived by a fixed naming rule unless the SKU code is given.
- **R-PPC-134 — order management is PPC's:** the order book, its upload, amendments, holds, short-close and internal orders are built in PPC only. The production-confirmation order upload stays as it is until PPC's order management is built; SAP and FP integration wait for their integration details (the sources SAP and FP of `mes_ppc_order_inbound`, parked).
- **R-PPC-135 — PPC's order upload:** the same format as today's Order Data upload — the columns read from the production-confirmation application at upload time — plus the PPC columns the requirements need: order category incl. MTO / MTS, ISO, NCO, trial, development and dummy; SAP order type and planning relevance; tolerance; customer priority; PSN and revision; delivery schedule; double rolling and as-cast sale; hold and short-close state (Annex A). PPC keeps the upload (`mes_ppc_order_upload`), one inbound row per sheet row (source UPLOAD) and the extra fields (`mes_ppc_order`, `mes_ppc_order_line`). Validation repeats the checks the production-confirmation upload makes where PPC can read the masters — customer, product and SKU, attribute values and ranges, duplicate order + line # + schedule line, TDC length — and adds PPC's own: category, SAP type, tolerance band, PSN and revision. An INVALID row is never forwarded.
- **R-PPC-136 — automatic forwarding:** PPC posts the standard columns of the valid rows as one .xlsx to `POST /api/orders/import`, as the PPC user; forwards run one at a time, never two in parallel. Every row is a **full row** — the import overwrites every field and blanks clear, so PPC sends the line's complete current values, never a delta; the **SKU code is explicit**; the **pending quantity is carried forward** — new quantity − quantity already made, where already made = the line's quantity − its pending quantity as the application holds them — because the import overwrites it. PPC reads the per-row result back (`forward_status`, `rejection_reason`), shows rejections in PPC and, on acceptance, matches the order and its lines and writes `mes_ppc_order` / `mes_ppc_order_line` (R-PPC-117; PPC_ORDER_UPLOAD_FORWARDED, PPC_ORDER_UPLOAD_REJECTED). The Allocation application gets its copy through the existing sync. When the import aborts as a whole — for example because the copy to the Allocation application fails — the forward is FAILED and retried.
- **R-PPC-137 — what the import cannot take:** an amendment of an order released in the planner is refused by the import; it stays in PPC — the PPC line keeps the new values with `forward_state` NOT_APPLIED, shown "not applied — order released" (PPC_ORDER_NOT_APPLIED). Cancel and short-close: PPC keeps the true status (`ppc_line_status`) and forwards status "closed" with pending 0. A hold: PPC forwards ON_HOLD for display and remembers the earlier status (`status_before_hold`), forwarded back on release. Orders PPC creates on its screens — internal, ISO, NCO, trial, development — are forwarded the same way (source PPC); a dummy order only once approved (R-PPC-107).
- Worked example — an order upload of 120 lines with 2 rejected: a planner uploads a 120-row sheet covering 34 orders. All 120 rows pass PPC's validation, and PPC forwards them in one file. The import accepts 118 rows and rejects 2 — lines 10 and 20 of SO-90117, an amendment from 300 t to 340 t of an order already released in the planner. PPC writes the side-table rows of the 118 accepted lines; the two lines of SO-90117 keep 340 t in PPC, shown "not applied — order released"; the upload ends PARTIAL — 118 accepted, 2 not applied. Among the accepted rows, SO-90231 / 10, raised from 500 t to 560 t with 180 t already made, was forwarded with pending 380 t.

### 21.8 Job work — PPC
- Facts (code): the dispatch challan (INTERNAL and FG only), the supplier return (it breaks the order link) and the Quality inward (it never creates a lot) cannot carry job work; the supplier master types are MILL / TRADER / IMPORT / blank.
- **R-PPC-138 — send-out:** a job-work operation per external process, with one equipment row per vendor (masters, through the existing master services); the lot reaches it by its route or by `/api/operation-assignment/assign`. PPC writes and prints its own challan (`mes_ppc_job_work_challan`, with OUT lines in `mes_ppc_job_work_movement`) and holds each sent lot through the existing lot hold with the reason "At vendor" — it cannot be charged or confirmed while out (PPC_JOB_WORK_SENT). Whole lots only (part-lot send-out: OPEN-PPC-17).
- **R-PPC-139 — receipt and confirmation:** on return PPC records the vendor's delivery challan and quantities (IN lines), releases the "At vendor" hold and posts `POST /api/pc/confirm` at the job-work operation — the vendor's equipment; the sent lot in; the returned lot out with its new size or code, quantity, pieces and order; scrap and losses (invisible loss computed, R-PPC-89); optionally a Quality inspection hold on the returned lot (PPC_JOB_WORK_RECEIVED). No change request. Later options: a JOB_WORK supplier type, a vendor challan from the dispatch module, part-lot send-out. The content of PPC-R-25 moves into PPC.
- Worked example: challan JW-0031 sends two lots, 50.0 t of RO 40 for peeling, to vendor A (equipment "Vendor A peeling" of the operation "Peeling — job work"); both lots are held "At vendor". The vendor returns 46.8 t against its delivery challan 7781, with 1.2 t of turnings and 0.4 t of scrap; PPC releases the holds and confirms the operation — in: 2 lots, 50.0 t; out: 1 lot, 46.8 t of the peeled size for the same order line; scrap 0.4 t; visible loss 1.2 t; invisible loss 1.6 t — and the challan is CONFIRMED.

Every PPC screen was built as a clickable prototype on sample data and the Functional Design was aligned with them (FDD 0.13). This section lands what the alignment made explicit in the data: new columns on PPC's own tables, added status values (amended in place above, marked 22.x) and the policies behind the new thresholds. Where a prototype had simplified the agreed design, the design stands: the tolerance matrix of R-PPC-06 and the grinding team's allocation of R-PPC-78 are unchanged. No new table; no production-confirmation table is touched. Rules continue from R-PPC-143.

### 22.1 Order book — PPC
- `mes_ppc_order` gains `approval_note varchar(500)` (Y — the approver's comment on a dummy order; mandatory on reject) · `marketing_priority_qty numeric(18,4)` (Y — the quantity Marketing prioritises for the period, from the MARKETING_PRIORITY sheet, beside `marketing_rank`).
- **R-PPC-144 — release quantity within the band:** a change of the release quantity within the band of the order-quantity class (R-PPC-06; seed ≤ 5 t ± 30 % · 5–10 t ± 20 % · > 10 t ± 10 %) applies at once and moves the balances to cast, roll and process by the change; a change beyond the band, and a release above the order quantity plus the band, wait for TOLERANCE_OVERRUN_APPROVE with the reason (`approval_status` PENDING) — nothing is refused. An increase of the order quantity uses the same band, and so does the FP-basis cap of R-PPC-99 (FP quantity ≤ balance + band). Worked example: 500 t ordered, 400 t released, band ± 50 t → 420 t applies (BTC 300 → 320 t); 480 t and 560 t wait for the HOD.
- **R-PPC-145 — amendments from the upload:** a quantity, date or status change arriving in PPC's order upload is written as an amendment with source UPLOAD, under the same band and approval as a change made on the screen.

### 22.2 Material — PPC
- `mes_ppc_sms_batch_feed` gains `match_reason varchar(500)` (Y — mandatory for a MANUAL match) · `heat_generated_at timestamptz` (Y) · `charged_at timestamptz` (Y) · `cut_at timestamptz` (Y — when each replay step was done; a step already done is not posted again on retry).
- `mes_ppc_batch_link_discrepancy` gains `feed_id` FK `mes_ppc_sms_batch_feed` (Y — the reported heat the discrepancy came from) · `piece_no int` (Y) · `outcome varchar(20)` (Y — OVERRIDDEN / LEFT_FREE / CORRECTED_BY_SMS, set when the status leaves OPEN).
- `mes_ppc_job_work_challan` gains `expected_back_date date` (Y — the challan is overdue once the date passes with lines not returned).
- `mes_ppc_job_work_movement` gains `returned_size varchar(30)` (Y — IN: the size as returned) · `returned_sku_id` FK `mes_skus` (Y — IN: the material code of the returned lot, the operation's suffix applied).
- **R-PPC-146 — matching by hand and discrepancies:** a planner may match a reported heat to a planned heat of the same grade and PSN — another caster is allowed — with a reason (`match_reason`); a grade or PSN mismatch is not offered. A hard discrepancy (section) stops the billet cut; a soft one (length within the policy tolerance, seed ± 100 mm) may be overridden with a reason; either may be left free (billets cut without an order, offered as free lots) or corrected when SMS MES re-reports; the outcome is stored. A retry is refused while a discrepancy of the heat is open.
- **R-PPC-147 — auto-swap draw:** candidates are drawn whole while the total stays within the required quantity plus the allowance (policy PPC_AUTOSWAP_ALLOWANCE_T, seed 0.5 t), and the search stops once less than PPC_AUTOSWAP_STOP_T (seed 1 t) remains; at an equal fit, free stock wins over a donor line. A failed Release rolls back the Un-Release and the block (`step_status` ROLLED_BACK).
- **R-PPC-148 — shortage window:** the shortage watch reads the released blocks starting within PPC_SHORTAGE_WINDOW_HOURS (seed 36); a block drops out once started; the alert is due at PPC_SHORTAGE_LEAD_HOURS before the start and sent once.
- **R-PPC-149 — job-work returns:** invisible loss = sent − output − visible loss − scrap; above PPC_JOBWORK_LOSS_REVIEW_PCT of what was sent (seed 3 %) the line is marked for review; a receipt whose output, visible loss and scrap exceed what was sent is refused; a partial return leaves the challan PART_RECEIVED. Only whole lots standing at the job-work operation (by route or next-operation correction) may be sent.
- **R-PPC-150 — route explanation:** the routing candidates a resolution did not choose are not stored; Explain re-derives them from the routing attributes pinned with the route.

### 22.3 Campaign planning, monthly plan and rolling sequence — PPC
- `mes_ppc_rolling_schedule_block` gains `cut_length_reason varchar(255)` (Y — mandatory when the BLM cut length is an ALTERNATE).
- **R-PPC-151 — monthly-plan versions:** each version of a mill's month is a row under the same plan code; only a DRAFT version is edited; Approve (PPC-HOD) sets the first version APPROVED and a later one AMENDED, and every earlier version SUPERSEDED; PPC_MONTHLY_PLAN_AMENDED is raised when the amended version is approved.
- **R-PPC-152 — review turns:** campaign and rolling-schedule rounds are rows of `mes_ppc_campaign_review`; the awaited party is the `to_role` of the latest row; a campaign is agreed when its latest decision is ACCEPT and no line, inclusion or basis changed after it (audit tail), so any change after agreement needs a new round; Finalise needs agreement and no open round. A schedule PPC counters is COUNTERED until the Mills propose again.
- **R-PPC-153 — feasibility outcomes:** an order of an exempt category or source gets EXEMPT results; an order failing sequence, section and family load beyond tolerance is DROPPED unless force-included; under the FP basis a line without an FP quantity is NOT_PLANNED.
- **R-PPC-154 — adding a size:** a size added to an accepted schedule needs the retrieval notice (6 h existing / 24 h new size) and the MoQ; short of either, SEQUENCE_SUPERVISOR_OVERRIDE with a reason; the change log records SIZE_ADD, LENGTH, RELEASE and MOVED_ACROSS_BLOCK changes.

### 22.4 Casting indent and downstream — PPC
- `mes_ppc_casting_indent` gains `plan_load_status varchar(15)` (DRAFT / LOADED / RE_OPENED — default DRAFT) · `plan_loaded_at timestamptz` (Y) · `plan_loaded_by bigint` (Y).
- `mes_ppc_casting_indent_line` gains `line_status varchar(15)` (DRAFT / LOADED / RE_OPENED — default DRAFT).
- `mes_ppc_abgm_schedule_line` gains `kept_out_reason varchar(255)` (Y — set at release for a lot on hold or whose code does not allow the step; the line stays out of the release).
- **R-PPC-155 — casting-plan load state:** Load as casting plan sets the loaded lines and the indent LOADED with the time and user; a rejected heat reported by SMS MES adds its weight to the line, recomputes the heats and sets the line and the indent RE_OPENED until the next load. Heats per line = BTC ÷ heat size rounded up, a remainder within 10 % of a heat spread over the others. Worked example: 250 t at 25 t → 10 heats; a heat of 24.6 t rejected → 274.6 t → 11 heats, line RE_OPENED.
- **R-PPC-156 — ABGM allocation and changes:** the grinding team allocates grinder and shift on the released schedule without approval (change log ALLOCATION, approval NOT_REQUIRED; the section-limit refusal applies; R-PPC-78); a change of sequence, size, grade or priority by the unit is PENDING until PPC-HOD approves. A hot-out line is ground without a code change (category HOT_OUT).
- **R-PPC-157 — downstream proposals:** a proposed change inside a released downstream schedule runs the checks; a BLOCK keeps it in the list as NOT_SUBMITTED; a PASS or WARN goes to PPC-HOD.

### 22.5 Ferro alloy, dashboard and masters — PPC
- **R-PPC-158 — masters:** an add, edit or deactivation of a Scheduling Constraints row is held by the platform approvals (D-03) until a checker — a PPC super-user other than the maker — approves it; a rejected change is not applied; deactivation sets `effective_to` to the day of approval and the audit tail's `active_status` inactive. Codes are unique; the parameter follows the type (number, text, select or master reference). Setting rows (CAMPAIGN_BASIS, HEAT_SIZE_T, CHARGING_POOL_MODE) carry no enforcement; SECTION_LOAD delegates to the section-load master like MOQ_RULE, FAMILY_LOAD carries its own tonnage. Sequence Templates are maintained by the PPC super-user with audit and no checker; one default per mill, and the default cannot be deactivated.
- **R-PPC-159 — thresholds as policies:** ferro-alloy variance within PPC_FEALLOY_VARIANCE_PCT (seed ± 3 %) is OK, above OVER, below UNDER; the dashboard marks a unit below PPC_DASHBOARD_AMBER_PCT (seed −4 %) amber and below PPC_DASHBOARD_RED_PCT (seed −8 %) red.

## 23. Gap points (27-Oct-2026)
Three gap points raised on the design, each closed inside PPC with no change request: the production-confirmation and Allocation applications are used through their existing services — the confirmation already consumes part of a lot and leaves the rest on the same lot; the lot hold, its release and the release block are unchanged.

### 23.1 Order holds — part release and who placed the hold *(gap points "not able to release partial order quantity" and "hold by Marketing in SAP / SFDC")* — PPC
- `mes_ppc_order_hold` gains `held_by_name varchar(100)` (Y — who placed the hold: the PPC user's name, or the function and person the source names, for example Marketing and the person's name) · `source_ref varchar(60)` (Y — the upload row or the SAP / SFDC message that placed it) · `freed_qty numeric(18,4)` (Y — the quantity not yet made that part releases of a LINE or ORDER hold freed) · `release_kind varchar(10)` (Y — WHOLE / SOURCE, set by the release that ends the hold).
- **`mes_ppc_order_hold_release`** — `hold_release_id` PK · `hold_id` FK `mes_ppc_order_hold` · **`release_kind varchar(10)`** (WHOLE / PART / SOURCE) · `lot_count int` (Y — lots released, no longer followed or no longer to be held) · `released_qty numeric(18,4)` (Y — PART: the quantity not yet made it freed) · `alloc_unblocked boolean` (Y — the release lifted the Allocation release block) · `reason varchar(500)` · `released_by bigint` (Y — blank when the source released it) · `released_by_name varchar(100)` (Y — the source's releaser, e.g. "Marketing · R. Menon") · `source_ref varchar(60)` (Y — the upload row or message that released it) · `released_at timestamptz` · **+ audit tail**. One row per release of a hold.
- `mes_ppc_hold_lot` gains `hold_release_id` FK `mes_ppc_order_hold_release` (Y — the release that released the lot).
- `mes_ppc_order_inbound` gains `hold_reason_code varchar(30)` (Y — the hold reason of the upload row or the SAP / SFDC message, mapped to the hold-reason master) · `hold_placed_by varchar(100)` (Y — who placed the hold, as the source names it).
- **R-PPC-160 — part release:** a single line's hold may be released in part — chosen lots (their lot holds released; a lot skipped for another hold no longer followed; a pending lot no longer to be held) and / or a quantity not yet made, at most what is held with no lot yet: for an UNRELEASED_BALANCE hold the release quantity rises by it, for a LINE or ORDER hold `freed_qty` rises by it. The hold stays open with what is left; the line shows PART HOLD. A freed quantity lifts the Allocation release block; PPC planning holds back the rest and a lot pegged beyond the freed quantity is held as it appears (R-PPC-57). Lots alone keep the block. When nothing is left held the release becomes WHOLE. A mass release releases whole holds only. Worked example: SO-91802 / 10, 600 t, line hold from the order upload: B031090 and B031091 (49.4 t) and 100 t not yet made released → PART HOLD with one lot applied, one skipped, one pending and 375.6 t held back; 4 plan entries unblocked.
- **R-PPC-161 — holds from the source:** a hold from the order upload, SAP or SFDC records its reason (mapped to the hold-reason master — seed row MARKETING "Hold by Marketing", applied at Order) and who placed it; the order upload carries `hold_reason_code` and `hold_placed_by` until the SAP and SFDC interfaces exist, then their hold and release messages do. When the source releases the hold PPC releases it as a whole (`release_kind` SOURCE). Under policy PPC_EXTERNAL_HOLD_RELEASE = ALLOWED_WITH_REASON (seed) the PPC Super User may also release it, whole or in part, with a reason — a whole release applies again if the next upload or message still carries the hold, a part release stands until the source releases it; SOURCE_ONLY leaves it to the source.

### 23.2 Job work — part receipts *(gap point "partial receipt; the remaining tons counted as invisible loss")* — PPC
- `mes_ppc_job_work_movement` gains `out_movement_id` FK `mes_ppc_job_work_movement` (Y — IN: the OUT line the receipt belongs to; an OUT line may have several IN lines) · `processed_pieces int` (Y — IN: pieces of the sent lot the receipt covers, returned or scrapped at the vendor) · `slice_qty numeric(18,4)` (Y — IN: the slice of the sent lot the confirmation consumed) · `is_last_receipt boolean` (Y — IN) · `remaining_qty numeric(18,4)` (Y — OUT: still at the vendor) · `remaining_pieces int` (Y — OUT).
- **R-PPC-162 — part receipts:** each receipt covers pieces of the sent lot, at most those still at the vendor; `slice_qty` = pieces × lot weight ÷ lot pieces, and the last receipt — flagged, or covering every piece left — takes `remaining_qty`, so no residue is stranded; a lot without pieces takes received + visible loss + scrap, its loss showing with the last receipt. PPC releases the At vendor hold, posts the confirmation with the sent lot consumed by `slice_qty` (the rest stays on the same lot at the job-work operation, with its order) and holds the rest At vendor again, storing the new hold on the OUT line (`inventory_hold_id`). `invisible_loss_qty` is computed per IN line on its slice (R-PPC-149 on the slice), the line's loss is their sum, and a receipt above its slice is refused. Worked example: R031020, 40.0 t in 100 pieces; 60 pieces → slice 24.0 t, 23.3 t received, 0.4 t turnings, 0.1 t scrap → 0.2 t; 16.0 t / 40 pieces remain At vendor; the last receipt takes 16.0 t → 0.2 t; line 0.4 t (1.0 %).
